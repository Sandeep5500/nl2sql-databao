# How text-to-SQL candidate selectors, verifiers and reward models are trained, and how their training data is built

Conventions used throughout:
- **Benchmark default.** Numbers are BIRD-dev execution accuracy (EX, %) unless stated otherwise.
- **MV / SC.** Majority voting and self-consistency over execution results.
- **Gap closed.** Defined as (selector − MV or SC) / (oracle pass@K − MV or SC). It is **my arithmetic**, not a figure any paper reports.
- **Measured vs claimed.** "Paper measured" means a table value. "Paper claims" means prose that no table backs.
- **Overlap flags.** These use the caller's list (BIRD train: movie_3≈Sakila, retail_world≈Northwind, works_cycles≈AdventureWorks, soccer_2016≈IPL; Spider-1 train: sakila_1, chinook_1, store_1, soccer_1, formula_1, baseball_1). Section 7 adds one new finding: overlap with BIRD **dev** databases.

## 1. CHASE-SQL's fine-tuned pairwise selection agent: exact data construction, size, and gain over zero-shot selection and self-consistency

### Takeaway
CHASE-SQL fine-tuned Gemini-1.5-Flash on about 3.8K pairwise examples. It used LoRA rank 16 for 10 epochs. The examples came from running its own three candidate generators on BIRD train and keeping only (correct cluster, incorrect cluster) pairs, with the order of the two shuffled.

Results:
- **Pair accuracy.** 71.01% binary accuracy, against 63.98% for an untuned Gemini-1.5-Pro. A tuned Gemma-2-9B reached only 64.28%.
- **End to end.** BIRD-dev EX went from 68.84 (SC) to 73.01, against an 82.79 oracle. That closes about 30% of the gap.
- **Not reported.** The paper gives no counts of majority overturns or false overrides.

### Cited Findings
- **What it judges.** The selector is pairwise (k=2). Its input is the question, the hint, a schema restricted to the union of tables and columns used by the two candidates, both SQL queries, and both execution results. It outputs "A" or "B" (Sec. 3.5, Alg. 3, App. A.8 prompt). — [CHASE-SQL, arXiv 2410.01943](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01943)
- **Base model and method.** The paper says: "a Gemini 1.5 Flash model ... on a dataset of 3.8K samples generated by running the candidate generators on the BIRD training dataset". It "is trained for 10 epochs using a LoRA adapter with a rank of 16 using Vertex AI tuning API" (App. A.2). This is SFT on a classification label. — [CHASE-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01943)
- **Data construction (Sec. 3.5).**
  - The authors "first generate candidate SQL queries on the training set" with their generators: divide-and-conquer CoT, query-plan CoT, and online-synthetic-example prompting.
  - Candidates are grouped into clusters by execution result.
  - Training tuples (Qu, Ci, Cj, Dij, yij) are built only where at least one cluster is correct and another is incorrect.
  - The order of the correct and incorrect query is randomly shuffled to avoid order bias.
  - "For instances where no correct candidate exists, we include the ground truth SQL query in the prompt as a hint to guide the model in generating correct candidates."
  - Source: [CHASE-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01943)
- **Not reported:** the positive/negative balance, the number of BIRD-train questions used, how many pairs come from each question, and whether both presentation orders of a pair appear in training. — [CHASE-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01943)
- **Inference-time algorithm.**
  - Every pair is scored in both orders, (ci, cj) and (cj, ci), "to mitigate any order bias".
  - If two candidates have the same execution result, one is marked the winner without calling the model.
  - The answer is the argmax of wins, and ties are broken arbitrarily.
  - The headline pool is 21 candidates (7 per generator).
  - Source: [CHASE-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01943)
- **Binary pair accuracy, measured (Table 5).** The test set is BIRD-dev pairs with one correct and one incorrect candidate. Claude-3.5-Sonnet 60.21; Gemini-1.5-Pro 63.98; tuned Gemma 2 9B 64.28; tuned Gemini-1.5-Flash 71.01. — [CHASE-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01943)
- **Conflicting zero-shot figure.** The prose in Sec. 3.5 says "the LLM without fine-tuning resulted in only 58.01% binary classification accuracy". This conflicts with Table 5's 63.98 for untuned Gemini-1.5-Pro, and the paper does not explain the difference. — [CHASE-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01943)
- **End-to-end BIRD-dev, measured.** SC 68.84; a zero-shot listwise "ranker agent" 65.51; pairwise selection agent 73.01; oracle upper bound 82.79. — [CHASE-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01943)
- **Per-generator results, measured (Table 6).** The selection agent beats SC in every setting, by 3.06 to 6.22 points:
  - QP at T=0.5: SC 65.78 → 71.70 (upper bound 78.55).
  - OS at T=0.5: SC 67.34 → 70.40 (upper bound 74.77).
  - The prose claims "roughly 6%".
  - Source: [CHASE-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01943)
- **Spider transfer.** CHASE-SQL reports 87.6 on Spider test "without modifying the few-shot samples in the prompts or training a new selection model", so the BIRD-trained selector was reused. There is no selector-only ablation on Spider. — [CHASE-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01943)
- **Failure breakdown (App. A.4, BIRD dev).** 72.9% correct; 10.4% where a correct candidate existed but was not chosen; 6.7% with no correct candidate; 10.0% with a wrong gold query. — [CHASE-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01943)
- **Leaderboard.** "CHASE-SQL + Gemini" (Google Cloud) scores 76.02 BIRD test and ranks 20th as of Oct 2026. — [BIRD leaderboard](https://bird-bench.github.io/)
- **Not released.** MaP-SQL had to re-implement CHASE-style pairwise selection "since neither CHASE-SQL nor R3-SQL has released their original models". — [MaP-SQL PDF, arXiv 2609.00834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00834)
- **Independent re-test of a CHASE-style SFT selector.** R³-SQL trained one on OmniSQL-7B: 67.66% binary accuracy against 78.86% for GRPO with a swap-consistency reward. End-to-end EX was 69.82 against 71.45. The pairs were generated by Qwen2.5-Coder-7B at T=0.8 (Tables 14–15). This confirms the caller's known R³ figures: 78.9 vs 67.7 pair accuracy, and +1.63 EX. — [R³-SQL PDF, arXiv 2604.25325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)

### Inferences
- **Gap closed.** (73.01 − 68.84) / (82.79 − 68.84) = **29.9%** of the SC-to-oracle gap (my arithmetic).
- **At 9B scale, CHASE's data was not enough.** The tuned Gemma-2-9B (64.28) only matched untuned Gemini-1.5-Pro (63.98) and stayed 6.7 points below tuned Flash. About 4K pairs from one benchmark is unlikely to be enough for a 9B judge to beat a strong zero-shot judge.
- **Cluster size is invisible to the selector.** Pairs are cluster representatives, and same-result pairs are never shown. The selector therefore learns "which of two different results is correct" and never "when to override a bigger cluster". That matches our minority-correct use case only by accident.
- **The gold-as-hint trick has a cost.** Injecting gold SQL to manufacture correct candidates raises pair yield, but those positives are off-policy relative to the generator's natural outputs.
- **Overlap flag.** Rebuilding CHASE-style data from BIRD train pulls in movie_3, retail_world, works_cycles and soccer_2016, which share schemas with Sakila, Northwind, AdventureWorks and IPL.

### Gaps
- No positive/negative ratio, per-question pair count, or count of training questions is reported. It is also not stated whether same-result or both-order duplicates appear in training.
- No count of cases where the selector overrode a correct majority, or rescued a minority-correct answer.
- The 58.01 vs 63.98 zero-shot discrepancy is unexplained.

## 2. Leaderboard pipeline selectors: how XiYan-SQL, Contextual-SQL, Agentar-Scale-SQL, CSC-SQL, OpenSearch-SQL, SIRIUS-SQL, R³-SQL and MSc-SQL built their selector data

### Takeaway
Almost every trained selector is built the same way:
1. Sample many candidates on BIRD train (sometimes Spider train too).
2. Label each by execution match against gold.
3. Pair or group correct against incorrect, usually across execution-result clusters.
4. Train a 7B–32B Qwen-family model with SFT or GRPO/DAPO.

Where they differ is negative mining:
- XiYan creates LLM-made near-miss negatives.
- Contextual uses the 15 highest-likelihood wrong candidates.
- R³ uses the top-15 highest-scoring wrong candidates.
- SIRIUS keeps both-wrong pairs (20.3% of its training pairs).

Gains over MV/SC are +1.8 to +4.2 EX, closing roughly 15–35% of the gap to oracle. Several trained selectors underperform MV when used alone, or on another generator's pool.

### Cited Findings

#### XiYan-SQL
- **v1 (arXiv 2411.08599).** The authors "fine-tune a selection model" and "deliberately perform paraphrasing on the training data of the selection model" to match candidates' syntactic preferences. In the measured ablation (Table 8), XiYan-SQL All scores 71.58; without the selection model it scores 68.84 (−2.74). The full system reaches 73.34 dev and 75.63 test. — [XiYan-SQL v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.08599)
- **v2 / multi-generator (arXiv 2507.04701; IEEE TKDE vol. 38, Apr 2026), what it judges.** Listwise. The model sees "the question, the union of all filtered schemas, the evidence, and the reorganized candidates". Execution results are used to cluster candidates before the call. — [XiYan-SQL multi-generator](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04701)
- **v2, base model and cost.** "Given that the selection task is relatively simpler than the generation task, we choose Qwen2.5-Coder-7B for developing the SQL selection model" (instruct version). It is "fine-tuned on constructed comparative samples". Fine-tuning takes "about 15 GPU hours" and about 50M tokens (A100s). — [XiYan-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04701)
- **v2, data construction.**
  - The goal is "a rich listwise comparison dataset".
  - Candidates come from multiple generators: the ensemble of fine-tuned models plus ICL models such as GPT-4o.
  - A positive is "any candidate that produces an identical execution result" to the gold query, not only an exact match to it.
  - Negatives are natural failures (syntax, execution and logical errors) plus "challenging 'near-miss' examples created via LLM-based 'controlled modifications'" that introduce subtle errors into correct queries.
  - All candidates are "de-formalized" with a SQL formatter, removing casing and spacing, so the model cannot key on generator style.
  - GPT-4o is also used "to augment the training data" for selection.
  - The full pipeline is in Appendix II.C, which is not in the arXiv PDF.
  - Source: [XiYan-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04701)
- **v2, inference reorganization.**
  1. Drop candidates that error during execution.
  2. Cluster by execution result. If only one cluster exists, return its shortest SQL.
  3. Otherwise sort clusters by size, and sort within a cluster by generator rank.
  4. If the largest cluster holds at least ⌈|L|/2⌉ candidates, pass all candidates in that order. Otherwise pass one shortest candidate per cluster.
  - Source: [XiYan-SQL multi-generator](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04701)
- **v2, standalone selection accuracy, measured (Table IX).** Qwen2.5-Coder-7B base 62.84; Gemini-1.5-pro 66.30; GPT-4o 67.47; fine-tuned selector 69.56. — [XiYan-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04701)
- **v2, end-to-end, measured (Table VIII, pl = 5 / 10 candidates).**
  - MV 69.94 / 70.21. The text says 69.82.
  - s1, random candidate order: 69.69 / 68.19.
  - s2, ordered by generator performance: 71.45 / 71.84.
  - s3, the proposed reorganization: 72.29 / 73.34.
  - The upper bound at pl=10 is 82.2. Using only MV costs 3.13 points (Table III).
  - Source: [XiYan-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04701)
- **v2, Spider.** Spider test is 89.65 with no Spider-specific tuning, and there is no selector-only Spider number. — [XiYan-SQL multi-generator](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04701)

#### Contextual-SQL (reward model `ctx-bird-reward-250121`)
- **Base model.**
  - The HF card says "Finetuned from Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct" (33B, BF16), and the blog says Qwen-2.5-32B.
  - R³-SQL and MaP-SQL instead describe the backbone as Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct. This is a conflict between the primary source and later citations.
  - Sources: [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/ContextualAI/ctx-bird-reward-250121); [Contextual AI blog](https://contextual.ai/blog/open-sourcing-the-best-local-text-to-sql-system); [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **What it judges.** It scores each candidate pointwise. The HF system prompt lists schema, question, external knowledge, SQL, a logprob score and the execution result. The example user message includes the row count and the result text. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/ContextualAI/ctx-bird-reward-250121)
- **Data construction (BIRD train).**
  - For each training question, "a large pool of distinct candidates" is sorted by generation likelihood.
  - Candidates are labelled positive or negative by execution match against gold.
  - Each question in a mini-batch gets 1 random positive plus the 15 highest-likelihood incorrect candidates.
  - The objective is to identify the correct SQL among those 16, a classification task with hard-negative mining.
  - Source: [Contextual AI blog](https://contextual.ai/blog/open-sourcing-the-best-local-text-to-sql-system); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/ContextualAI/ctx-bird-reward-250121)
- **Inference.**
  - Generator: Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct at temperature 1, with n samples for each of m few-shot prompts. A pass@k study used 32 × 32 = 1,024 candidates.
  - Candidates with execution errors are filtered out.
  - The final score is a weighted average of the generator's cumulative log-prob and the RM score, with α = 0.4 chosen by a sweep on a dev subset.
  - Source: [Contextual AI blog](https://contextual.ai/blog/open-sourcing-the-best-local-text-to-sql-system)
- **Results.** Reward-only selection "already achieves ... 70%" on BIRD-dev, and log-probs plus reward give "around 73%" (claimed in the blog). The HF card lists dev 73.50 and test 75.63; the system was #1 in Feb 2025. No numeric MV baseline is given. — [Contextual AI blog](https://contextual.ai/blog/open-sourcing-the-best-local-text-to-sql-system); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/ContextualAI/ctx-bird-reward-250121)

#### Agentar-Scale-SQL (Ant Group; BIRD test 81.67)
- **What it judges.** Pairwise. The selector "determines a winner based on the question, light schema, and execution results". The prompt also lists matched DB contents and evidence. — [Agentar-Scale-SQL, arXiv 2509.24403](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24403)
- **Base model and method.** Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct trained with GRPO and a binary reward: 1 if the selection is correct, 0 otherwise. Training ran on verl with 32 A100s. No SFT stage is mentioned. — [Agentar-Scale-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24403)
- **Data.** "Based on the training set, we construct 8.5k samples for reinforcement learning." The paper does not report which generators produced the candidates, the positive/negative balance, hard-negative selection, or how position bias was handled. — [Agentar-Scale-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24403)
- **Inference.** Candidates are grouped by identical execution result and one representative is kept per group. A pairwise round-robin tournament follows, and the argmax of wins is submitted. The pool is 9 ICL + 8 reasoning = 17 candidates. — [Agentar-Scale-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24403)
- **Results, measured.** The full system scores 74.90 dev. Replacing the selector with SC gives 73.08, so the selector adds **+1.82**. The oracle is 84.29 (ICL generator alone 81.36, reasoning generator alone 75.88); test is 81.67. No standalone selector accuracy is reported. — [Agentar-Scale-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24403)

#### CSC-SQL (Corrective Self-Consistency): a merge-reviser, not a pure selector
- **What it does.**
  - SC picks the top-2 execution groups.
  - If they disagree, a "merge revision" model sees both SQL queries, the merged schema and both execution results, then generates 8 new SQL queries.
  - SC is applied again to those to pick the answer.
  - Source: [CSC-SQL, arXiv 2505.13271](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13271)
- **Model and training.** The merge reviser is Qwen2.5-Coder-3B or 7B-Instruct, trained with GRPO through TRL. Settings: lr 3e-6, 1 epoch, 6 completions, reward R_EX + 0.1·R_format. — [CSC-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13271)
- **Data.** Built from BIRD train. Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct and its GRPO variant each sampled 8 SQL queries per question. Groups were formed by execution result, and "the top two groups" became the merge-revision dataset. The example count is not stated. — [CSC-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13271)
- **Results, measured (n=64).**
  - Qwen2.5-Coder-7B: SC 64.45 → 68.70.
  - GRPO-tuned 7B: 66.15 → 69.19.
  - XiYanSQL-32B: 68.95 → 71.33.
  - Qwen2.5-Coder-32B: 68.67 → 70.69.
  - Llama-3.1-70B (n=4): 65.58 → 66.30.
  - Spider test (XiYanSQL-3B): 78.06 → 79.37.
  - Source: [CSC-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13271)

#### OpenSearch-SQL: no trained selector
- **Selection is voting.**
  - Unfixable and empty-result SQL is excluded first.
  - Then "choose the option with the highest output consistency in the execution results", with ties broken by shortest execution time.
  - The pool is 21 candidates from a single prompt, and "no fine-tuning" is used.
  - Source: [OpenSearch-SQL, arXiv 2502.14913](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14913)
- **Results, measured.** Dev is 67.80 without voting and 69.30 with it; test is 72.28. On Mini-Dev, voting adds +2.4, and +7.64 on hard questions. — [OpenSearch-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14913)

#### SIRIUS-SQL (arXiv 2606.01246, May 2026): a trained pairwise judge with execution results
- **Model and output.** The pairwise reward model is a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-Coder-32B. It takes two candidate SQL queries with their execution results and emits **two independent correctness labels** (`<sql1_judge>`, `<sql2_judge>`), which gives four classes. — [SIRIUS-SQL PDF, App. E.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01246)
- **Training.**
  - SFT warm-start: 3 epochs, lr 1e-5.
  - Then DAPO: 16 rollouts, 1,000 steps, KL disabled, with an exact-match reward of 1 only if both labels are right.
  - Source: [SIRIUS-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01246)
- **Data.**
  - All examples come from BIRD.
  - Candidates come from "several prompting strategies" (not named), are each executed to get a correctness tag, and become ordered pairs.
  - The train set is **79,800 pairs**: (c,c) 7,298 (9.2%), (c,i) 28,166 (35.3%), (i,c) 28,166 (35.3%), (i,i) 16,170 (20.3%).
  - Validation has 1,042 pairs, with no (i,i) pairs by construction.
  - The authors say the judge must stay accurate when both candidates are wrong.
  - Keeping both orderings of every mixed pair "prevents the judge from learning a position shortcut", and selection uses position-debiased verdicts that swap and combine.
  - Source: [SIRIUS-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01246)
- **Selector ablation, measured (BIRD dev; Hybrid pool with Gemini-3.1 Pro as the generalist; overall then challenging).**

  | Selector | Overall EX | Challenging |
  |---|---|---|
  | freq | 74.97 | – |
  | MV | 75.03 | 62.07 |
  | RM alone | 74.32 | 58.62 |
  | merge (S_maj + S_rm) | 75.68 | – |
  | merge + gate | 75.88 | – |

  The authors say: "RM alone is the weakest single signal ... because pairwise preference ranks representatives within a cluster of execution-equivalent candidates rather than discriminating across clusters." — [SIRIUS-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01246)
- **Gate.** The gate fires on about 5–10% of dev questions (K_max=6, T_max=8). The system scores 91.20 on Spider test. — [SIRIUS-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01246)

#### R³-SQL (arXiv 2604.25325, Findings ACL 2026)
- **What it judges.**
  - A pairwise ranker, R3-7B (called listwise with n=2), sees the schema, the question, and two candidates with their execution results.
  - A pointwise ranker, R3-Point-32B, adds a utility term weighted by group size.
  - Groups are execution-equivalent clusters.
  - Source: [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **Pairwise ranker training.**
  - Base model: OmniSQL-7B, which is itself trained on BIRD, Spider and SynSQL-2.5M.
  - Method: GRPO with a swap-consistency reward R = R_base + 0.5·R_c, where R_c = 1 when the decision survives swapping the order.
  - Data: "32 SQL candidates from BIRD-train and Spider-train, then sampling one correct and one incorrect candidate per question". Hard negatives are sampled "from the Top 15 highest-scoring incorrect candidates".
  - Context is 8K tokens, with execution results truncated proportionally.
  - The generator of the training candidates and the pair count are not stated.
  - Source: [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **Pointwise ranker.** It is initialized from Contextual-RM-32B and further trained on BIRD-train with "hard negative SQL candidates sorted by R3-7B". — [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **Results, measured.**
  - **Pair accuracy (Table 14).** OmniSQL-7B: SFT 67.66, GRPO 76.08, GRPO + consistency 78.86. Qwen2.5-Coder-7B: GRPO 74.22, GRPO + consistency 76.85.
  - **End-to-end (Table 15).** OmniSQL-7B: SFT 69.82, GRPO 70.79, GRPO + consistency 71.45.
  - **Order robustness.** The consistency objective improves input-order robustness by +11.89 pp.
  - Source: [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **Selection-algorithm comparison (shared selector model).**
  - Pool: Arctic-Text2SQL-R1-32B, T=0.8, n=32, plus resampling.
  - BIRD-dev: R³ 75.03; CHASE-SQL 73.34; Contextual-SQL 73.14; XiYan-SQL 72.03; CSC-SQL 71.58.
  - On an OmniSQL-7B pool (Table 16): R³ 71.83; Contextual 70.80; CHASE 68.38; XiYan 68.06; CSC 67.40.
  - Candidate recall (the oracle) is 84.62 with resampling and 81.23 without.
  - MaP-SQL says R³'s protocol "uses a shared selector to isolate the contribution of the selection algorithm". These rows therefore compare algorithms, not the original trained models.
  - Sources: [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325); [MaP-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00834)

#### MSc-SQL (arXiv 2410.12916; Layer 6)
- **What it judges.** A listwise "multi-sample critic". It takes the question, the reduced schema, and n candidates, each with its execution result or error and metadata. It "predict[s] the correct sample index as the next token". Main results use 2–3 candidates. — [MSc-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12916)
- **Models and training.** All models are under 10B: Mistral-7B-v0.3, Llama-3-8B, Gemma-2-9B. Training used QLoRA with r=32, α=128 on one A6000. The critic's training data is described only as "the same dataset" used for a single-candidate Llama-8B baseline; no split, count or balance is given. — [MSc-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12916)
- **Results, measured (BIRD dev, Table 5).**
  - No critique: 61.3.
  - Off-the-shelf Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B, pointwise: 60.2. This is worse than no critique.
  - Fine-tuned single-candidate Llama-8B: 62.9.
  - SC: 62.6.
  - Multi-sample critic: 65.6.
  - Oracle: 71.4.
  - Spider test: 84.7.
  - Source: [MSc-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.12916)

### Inferences
- **Gap closed, my arithmetic.**

  | System | Selector vs baseline | Oracle | Gap closed |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | XiYan v2 | 73.34 vs MV 70.21 | 82.2 | 26.1% |
  | Agentar | 74.90 vs SC 73.08 | 84.29 | 16.2% |
  | MSc-SQL | 65.6 vs SC 62.6 | 71.4 | 34% |
  | CHASE | 73.01 vs SC 68.84 | 82.79 | 29.9% |

  Published trained selectors close about **15–35%** of the vote-to-oracle gap on BIRD-dev.
- **Our zero-shot judge already sits above that band.** Our internal memory note records 296/540 for the 35B thinking judge, against MV 261 and pass@4 346 across four run sets. That closes about **41%** of the gap. Pools differ (4 candidates against 17–64, and a different benchmark), so this is directional only. — [internal memory note](/Users/deepikapalagani/.claude/projects/-Users-deepikapalagani-Documents-nl2sql-databao/memory/nl2sql-judge-selection-findings.md)
- **Cluster size matters.**
  - The strongest results feed the judge vote-count information or combine it with votes. Examples are XiYan's cluster-size ordering, SIRIUS's S_maj + S_rm, R³'s group-size utility, and Contextual's mix of log-prob and reward.
  - Vote-blind judges used alone underperformed MV twice: SIRIUS's RM alone, and XiYan's s1 at pl=10 (68.19 vs MV 70.21).
- **Fine-tuning helps pair accuracy far more than end-to-end EX.** R³ gained +11.2 pair-accuracy points for +1.63 EX. XiYan's fine-tuned 7B beat GPT-4o by only +2.09 selection points.

### Gaps
- **Unreported data details.**
  - XiYan: Appendix II.C, with counts, generator mix and near-miss rate, is not in the arXiv PDF.
  - Agentar: which generators produced the 8.5K samples, and their balance.
  - R³: the training generator and the pair count.
  - SIRIUS: the number of BIRD questions and which prompting strategies were used.
  - MSc-SQL: the critic's training split and size.
- **Unreported results.** No standalone pair accuracy is given for the Agentar or SIRIUS judges.

## 3. Reward models, verifiers and critics: GradeSQL, Reward-SQL, Graph-Reward-SQL, SQLCritic, ExCoT, LEVER, RuCo-C

### Takeaway
GradeSQL is the most useful released recipe. It trains a pointwise Yes/No outcome reward model (ORM) with LoRA on OmniSQL-7B candidates from BIRD and Spider train, at 82,640 and 50,073 labelled candidates respectively. It releases those candidates under Apache-2.0. It gains +1.95 over MV on BIRD-dev and +2.00 on Spider-test, and notably works best with SQL only, without execution results.

LEVER is the clearest evidence on two points: execution results help a small verifier (+3.0 on Spider), and verifiers trained on one LLM's samples transfer poorly to a weaker LLM's samples. Reward-SQL, Graph-Reward-SQL, ExCoT, SQLCritic and RuCo-C are mostly generator-side rewards or critics, but their pair and negative constructions can be reused.

### Cited Findings

#### GradeSQL (arXiv 2509.01308; workshop version arXiv 2606.30851, SURGeLLM at ACL 2026)
- **What it judges.** Pointwise. The best prompt is "Question: {db_schema + question} SQL: {sql} Is the SQL correct?", and the score is P("Yes"). Variants that add execution results (Data-only, Data+SQL) were worse; the paper says "excessive context appears to reduce the verifier's discriminative ability". — [GradeSQL v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01308v2)
- **Models and training.**
  - Backbones: Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B, Granite-3.3-2B/8B, Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3.1-8B and OmniSQL-7B. OmniSQL-7B is best.
  - Method: LoRA with the causal-LM loss on the Yes/No token. A BCE classification head was worse (66.95 vs 68.90).
  - Larger ORMs did not help: 14B 69.23, 32B 68.19.
  - Source: [GradeSQL v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01308v2)
- **Data.**
  - BIRD train: 9,411 questions after dedup. Spider train: 8,960.
  - OmniSQL-7B generated N=32 candidates per question.
  - A candidate is correct if its result set matches gold; execution errors are discarded.
  - BIRD: 82,640 candidates (41.37% incorrect / 58.63% correct); balanced to 30,686.
  - Spider: 50,073 (30.60% / 69.40%); balanced to 17,834.
  - Balancing keeps min(s+, s−) per question.
  - No other hard-negative mining is used.
  - Source: [GradeSQL v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01308v2)
- **Results, measured (N=32).**

  | Method | BIRD dev | Spider dev | Spider test |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Greedy | 63.89 | 82.40 | 84.02 |
  | MV | 66.95 | 83.75 | 85.47 |
  | Execution-based BoN | 66.04 | 82.79 | 85.14 |
  | ORM | 68.90 | 84.53 | 87.47 |
  | pass@32 | 80.57 | 91.68 | 93.29 |

  The Spider-dev gain "does not retain significance after Bonferroni correction". — [GradeSQL v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01308v2)
- **Off-generator results, measured (ORM trained on OmniSQL-7B candidates).**
  - OmniSQL-14B pool: ORM 69.04 vs MV 67.01.
  - OmniSQL-32B pool: ORM 69.30 vs MV 63.95.
  - By difficulty (simple / moderate / challenging): MV 72.86 / 59.70 / 52.41 vs ORM 74.15 / 62.93 / 54.48.
  - Source: [GradeSQL v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01308v2)
- **Gap closed, my arithmetic.** BIRD-dev: (68.90 − 66.95) / (80.57 − 66.95) = 14.3%. Spider-test: 25.6%.

#### Reward-SQL (arXiv 2505.04671; several versions)
- **v1 (May 2025).** A 7B process reward model (PRM) with a GRPO-aligned Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct policy reaches 68.9% BIRD dev, using PRM-guided best-of-N. — [Reward-SQL v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04671v1)
- **Latest version, PRM design.**
  - The PRM is Qwen2.5-7B-Coder-Instruct.
  - It sees the question, schema and Chain-of-CTE steps, each with its execution output.
  - Step labels come from MCTS rollouts: 8 per question at T=1.0, over 16,248 instances, trained with BCE.
  - The policy is Qwen3-8B, cold-started on 51,996 CoCTE trajectories that DeepSeek-V3.1 generated from 17,462 BIRD + Spider SQL queries.
  - Source: [Reward-SQL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04671)
- **Latest version, results.** BIRD dev: greedy 66.0; SC 66.6; ORM 67.1; PRM@8 68.7 (ablation); PRM@32 70.3. Spider test: greedy 81.2 → PRM@32 85.4. — [Reward-SQL HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04671)

#### Graph-Reward-SQL (arXiv 2505.12380; Findings EMNLP 2025)
- **What GMNScore is.** SQL is converted to Apache Calcite relational-operator trees and compared by a graph matching network, with no execution. — [Graph-Reward-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12380)
- **Data.**
  - 17,664 Spider-train SQL pairs with equivalence labels.
  - Plus 3,397 pairs from 7 augmentations. Equivalent pairs use rewrites such as IN-clause replacement. Non-equivalent pairs use AND↔OR swaps and comparison-operator swaps.
  - Source: [Graph-Reward-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12380)
- **Use and results.** It is used only as an RL reward, not for best-of-N.
  - AUC on Spider-dev / BIRD-dev pairs: EX 96.37 / 92.67; Bradley-Terry RM 89.24 / 84.77; GMNScore 97.62 / 94.14.
  - GMNScore is 3.99M parameters and takes 0.023 s per sample.
  - Source: [Graph-Reward-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12380)

#### SQLCritic (arXiv 2503.07996)
- **Model and output.** A clause-wise critic on Qwen-2.5-14B that takes the question and SQL and outputs (clause, error explanation) tuples. Training is SFT (3 epochs) then DPO with an adaptive β. — [SQLCritic v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07996v4)
- **Data.**
  - Built from BIRD.
  - Wrong SQL comes from CodeS and Qwen2.5 sampled at high temperature, filtered by skeleton distance to gold.
  - GPT-4 writes clause-wise critiques against the gold query.
  - A critique becomes "preferred" only if another LLM's rewrite, using only that critique, matches gold execution.
  - A random incorrect critique is the "dispreferred" one.
  - Source: [SQLCritic v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07996v4)
- **Benchmark.** SQLCriticBench has 3,019 samples. — [SQLCritic v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07996v4)
- **Results, measured (correction use, not selection).** BIRD: GPT-4o 49.87 → 54.58; CodeS-7B 57.17 → 59.34. — [SQLCritic v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07996v4)

#### ExCoT (arXiv 2503.19988; Snowflake): generator DPO, but useful pair rules
- **Off-policy pairs.** GPT-4o generates up to 32 CoT solutions per question. Pairs are chosen with the "largest discrepancies" in edit distance. — [ExCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19988)
- **On-policy pairs.** The model's own 32 samples, with pairs chosen at **smaller** edit distance (near-misses). — [ExCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19988)
- **Data and stages.** BIRD train (9.2k) + Spider train (8.6k). Stages are SFT, then 1 off-policy DPO round, then 2 on-policy rounds. The valid pool shrinks from more than 2,000 to about 560. — [ExCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19988)
- **Results, measured.** LLaMA-3.1-70B: 57.37 → 68.51 BIRD dev, 78.81 → 86.59 Spider test. No SC comparison. — [ExCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19988)

#### LEVER (arXiv 2302.08468; ICML 2023)
- **Verifier and input.** The Spider verifier is T5-base: 82.0, against 81.9 for T5-large and 83.1 for T5-3B. Its input is the NL question, the SQL and the linearized result table, with errors shown as "ERROR: [reason]". — [LEVER (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.08468)
- **Data.**
  - Spider train, 7,000 questions.
  - 20 Codex samples per question at T=0.6, and 50 at test time.
  - A sample is positive if its execution result matches gold, and the gold program is added as an extra positive.
  - 68.0% of Codex samples are positive.
  - Source: [LEVER (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.08468)
- **Scoring.** The score is P_LM × P_verifier, summed over programs with the same execution result. — [LEVER (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.08468)
- **Results, measured (Spider dev).** Greedy 75.3; execution-pruned max-likelihood (EP+ML) 77.3; LEVER 81.9. Removing execution information costs 3.0 points with Codex, and with InCoder drops 54.1 → 48.5. — [LEVER (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.08468)
- **Cross-LLM transfer, measured (Table 8a).** Each row is the LLM whose samples are being reranked; each column is the LLM whose samples trained the verifier.

  | Target LLM | Codex-trained | InCoder-trained | CodeGen-trained |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Codex | 82.0 | 81.7 | 80.8 |
  | InCoder | 46.4 | 54.1 | 47.6 |
  | CodeGen | 44.7 | 48.9 | 51.0 |

  Source: [LEVER (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2302.08468)

#### RuCo-C (arXiv 2511.22258)
- **Model and output.** A generative critic on Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (and 14B). It takes the question, schema and SQL and outputs a critique, a True/False verdict and corrected SQL. Training is SFT then GRPO. — [RuCo-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22258)
- **Data.**
  - SFT: Spider-train questions paired with erroneous SQL from Chen et al. 2023. About 40,000 examples were built and 15,000 used.
  - RL: BIRD train, with negatives from several text-to-SQL models labelled by test-suite accuracy plus limited manual checks.
  - Source: [RuCo-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22258)
- **Results, measured (7B; AUC / ACC / F1).** BIRD 72.40 / 68.29 / 54.04; Spider 68.15 / 68.07 / 67.33. — [RuCo-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22258)

### Inferences
- **Execution results: mixed evidence.** LEVER found they help a T5-base verifier (+3.0 on Spider). GradeSQL found SQL-only best for LLM ORMs. Our own note found that adding database context to the judge prompt helped easy pairs and hurt hard ones. Whether to include a result preview should be ablated, not assumed.
- **On-policy data is likely required.** LEVER shows a verifier trained on a stronger model's samples loses 7.7 points on InCoder's samples (54.1 → 46.4), while a weak-to-strong transfer loses only 0.3 (82.0 → 81.7 on Codex). A judge for our Qwen3.6-35B agent should be trained on that agent's own (or similar) candidates.
- **Released data overlaps our databases.** GradeSQL's datasets include BIRD train and Spider train, so they include the overlapping schemas. Filter by db_id before use.

### Gaps
- **Unreported data details.** GradeSQL's sampling temperature, and the generators behind Reward-SQL's PRM labels in v1 versus later versions.
- **No cross-dataset result for GradeSQL.** No paper reports training the ORM on BIRD and testing it on Spider.

## 4. Other 2025–2026 BIRD / Spider 2.0 leaderboard systems: who trains a selector

### Takeaway
Among published top systems, only Agentar-Scale-SQL, SIRIUS-SQL (Tencent; probably the "SiriusAI-SQL" leaderboard entry), XiYan-SQL, Contextual-SQL, R³-SQL and CHASE-SQL train a selector or reward model. CSC-SQL trains a merge reviser rather than a selector. DeepEye-SQL, AskData and OpenSearch-SQL explicitly avoid fine-tuning. The 2026 MaP-SQL and DPC papers argue for training-free selection. The top 20 entries on the Spider 2.0-Lite leaderboard are mostly agent products without papers, and none was found to train a selector.

### Cited Findings
- **BIRD leaderboard, overall (Oct 2026).** AskData (AT&T) 83.40; GrainSQL 82.95; DataGallery (Huawei) 82.39; SiriusAI-SQL (Tencent Data Computing Platform Dept.) 82.28; Agentar-Scale-SQL 81.67; Sber 81.33; Xiaomi 80.83. Most have no paper. — [BIRD leaderboard](https://bird-bench.github.io/)
- **BIRD single-trained-model track.** Gemini-SQL2 80.04; ReToolSQL (JPMorganChase, 31B) 78.14; SiriusAI-SQL-27B 77.70; SiriusAI-SQL-9B 77.08. An entry called "flash-sqlite: Qwen3.5-2B Generator + Self-Judge (SC@32)" scores 73.73 with no description. — [BIRD leaderboard](https://bird-bench.github.io/)
- **AskData's paper.** Its BIRD submission "does not use any specially tuned model" and relies on profiling with GPT-4o. This describes its 2024–2025 entries. — [arXiv 2505.19988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19988)
- **DeepEye-SQL.** It uses "confidence-aware selection that leverages execution-guided adjudication ... beyond simple majority voting", on about 30B-A3B MoE models "without any fine-tuning". BIRD dev 73.5, test 75.07; Spider test 89.8. — [DeepEye-SQL, arXiv 2510.17586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17586)
- **SOMA-SQL (Oracle; Spider 2.0-Lite #5 at 72.02).** It uses targeted probing queries, guided by an ambiguity taxonomy and disagreement among candidates, as evidence for final selection. The abstract does not say whether the selector is trained. — [SOMA-SQL, arXiv 2606.11424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11424); [Spider 2.0 leaderboard](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)
- **ReFoRCE (Spider 2.0-Lite 55.21 with o3).** Instead of selecting randomly after voting, it "retain[s] ambiguous examples for further refinement". — [ReFoRCE, arXiv 2502.00675](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00675)
- **Spider 2.0 maintainers' guidance (2025-01-07).** They "do not recommend using the Spider 2.0 Gold SQL we released for SFT". — [Spider 2.0 leaderboard/news](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)
- **MaP-SQL (Findings EMNLP 2026).** A training-free listwise selector. It retrieves "structured memories" distilled from training-split question/gold pairs and aggregates rankings over permutations of the input order. It argues that "fine-tuning listwise selectors is costly" because each example carries many SQL queries and execution results. — [MaP-SQL, arXiv 2609.00834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00834)

### Inferences
- **Unconfirmed link.** SiriusAI-SQL on the leaderboard and the SIRIUS-SQL paper share the Tencent affiliation. The leaderboard entry does not cite the paper, so treat the link as likely but unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No technical reports were found for GrainSQL, DataGallery, Sber, Xiaomi, RAS, MarkovSQL, Gemini-SQL2 or ReToolSQL, so it is unknown whether they train selectors.

## 5. Does a selector trained on BIRD, Spider-1 or synthetic data transfer to Spider 2.0 or other unseen benchmarks?

### Takeaway
Direct Spider 2.0 evidence is a single small table. In R³-SQL's Spider2.0-SQLite comparison, R³ scores 29.17 against 12.50–20.83 for the other selection methods, but the values are all multiples of 1/24, suggesting a small subset. Evidence on other unseen benchmarks is mixed:
- A BIRD-only pointwise reward model is "less reliable on unseen domains" (R³).
- Contextual-RM-32B falls below MV on Spider-test, but helps on EHRSQL (MaP-SQL).
- Generators trained on BIRD/Spider score only 13–16% on the 135-question Spider2.0-SQLite set.

### Cited Findings
- **R³-SQL Table 17 (Spider2.0-SQLite).** CSC-SQL 12.50; Contextual-SQL 16.67; CHASE-SQL 20.83; XiYan-SQL 16.67; R³-SQL 29.17. It follows "the evaluation protocol of Yao et al. (2025)". The question count is not stated. — [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **The protocol R³ cites.** In Yao et al. (Arctic-Text2SQL-R1), Spider2.0-SQLite is **135 questions**, the same local split we use. Greedy results:
  - Arctic-R1: 7B 15.6, 14B 16.3, 32B 16.3.
  - OmniSQL: 7B 13.3, 14B 12.6, 32B 14.8.
  - GPT-4o: 17.0.
  - Arctic's training data is BIRD-train (8,017), Spider-train (6,972), Spider-dev (985) and Gretel-Synth (11,811).
  - Source: [Arctic-Text2SQL-R1, arXiv 2505.20315](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20315)
- **R³, in-domain vs out-of-domain (Table 12).** Columns: BIRD-dev / Spider-test / Spider-DK / EHRSQL / ScienceBenchmark.
  - Full R³: 75.03 / 87.19 / 77.92 / 46.30 / 66.82.
  - Without the BIRD-only pointwise ranker: 74.19 / 86.71 / 76.82 / **46.97 / 67.28**.
  - The authors say "the pointwise scores are less reliable on unseen domains" because R3-Point-32B "is fine-tuned exclusively on BIRD", whereas OmniSQL's corpus is broader.
  - Source: [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **MaP-SQL: Contextual-RM-32B vs MV on new pools (n = 8 / 32).** The RM is trained on BIRD; the generators differ from its training pool.

  | Benchmark (generator) | MV | Contextual-RM-32B |
  |---|---|---|
  | BIRD-dev (Agentar-32B) | 71.38 / 70.93 | 71.45 / 71.71 |
  | BIRD-dev (Arctic-R1-7B) | 70.08 / 69.95 | 70.27 / 70.47 |
  | **Spider-test** (Arctic-R1-7B) | 87.07 / 87.02 | **86.88 / 86.72** |
  | EHRSQL (Arctic-R1-7B) | 38.10 / 39.59 | 38.79 / 43.52 |

  A zero-shot CHASE-style pairwise selector (Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B) also fell below MV on Spider-test (86.83 / 86.63) and on the BIRD Arctic pool (69.30 / 68.12). — [MaP-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00834)
- **CHASE-SQL and XiYan-SQL on Spider 1.0.** CHASE reused its BIRD-trained selector on Spider test (87.6). XiYan reports 89.65 "with no Spider-specific tuning". Neither isolates the selector's contribution. — [CHASE-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01943); [XiYan-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.04701)
- **RuCo-C.** Trained on BIRD; tested on Spider (ACC 68.07) and Spider-DK (77.84), which the authors present as generalization. — [RuCo-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22258)
- **Contextual-SQL.** No Spider results. The blog cites other work showing GPT-4o falls from 86.6% on Spider 1.0 to 10.1% on Spider 2.0, and lists Spider 2.0 as future work. — [Contextual AI blog](https://contextual.ai/blog/open-sourcing-the-best-local-text-to-sql-system)
- **HarnessSQL (arXiv 2610.12274, Oct 2026; abstract and secondary coverage only).** It trains agents inside an execution harness ("hidden execution oracles"): SFT on verified teacher trajectories, then execution-reward RL. Qwen3-14B goes from 22.2 to 54.8 on Spider 2.0-SQLite, and Qwen3-8B from 15.5 to 45.2. Both reportedly transfer to BIRD-Interact and LiveSQLBench. — [papers.cool listing](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.12274); [Digg summary](https://digg.com/ai/gty2yfyb)

### Inferences
- **R³'s Spider 2.0 subset is probably small.** Its Table 17 values (12.50, 16.67, 20.83, 29.17) are all multiples of 1/24 and none are k/135. It therefore likely used a subset (n = 24, 48, …, 120), not the full 135. If n=24, the spread between methods is 1–4 questions. This is my arithmetic and needs confirmation from the authors.
- **Expect partial transfer only.** A judge trained only on BIRD-train pairs should be expected to transfer partly to a new candidate distribution. MaP shows a BIRD reward model slightly below MV on Spider-test, while R³ shows a BIRD-only pointwise ranker hurting EHRSQL. Training data that is diverse in domain (OmniSQL-style SynSQL) or on-policy (our agent's candidates) is the documented mitigation.

### Gaps
- No paper measures a BIRD-, Spider- or synthetic-trained **selector** on the full 135-question Spider 2.0-Lite local split with the official evaluator.
- The HarnessSQL training/test separation for Spider 2.0-SQLite is unverified.

## 6. Minority-correct cases: do trained selectors overturn wrong majorities, and at what false-override cost?

### Takeaway
No paper on a trained selector reports how often it overturns a wrong majority or how often it overrides a correct one. The only explicit split comes from the training-free DPC:
- On majority-incorrect questions, SC 0% → DPC 21.4%.
- On majority-correct questions, 100% → 97.0%.
- Net gain: +1.2.

The best indirect evidence says a trained pairwise judge used alone loses to majority voting, especially on hard questions: SIRIUS's RM scored 74.32 vs 75.03 overall and 58.62 vs 62.07 on challenging questions. Gains come when the judge is combined with vote counts.

### Cited Findings
- **DPC (training-free).** It duels the largest cluster (champion) against the second-largest (challenger) on a "Minimal Distinguishing Database".
  - Data: BIRD Mini-Dev, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, N=5 (Table 4).
  - Overall: SC 46.4 → DPC 47.6.
  - **Majority-Correct: 100.0 → 97.0.**
  - **Majority-Incorrect: 0.0 → 21.4.**
  - Pass@5 is 57.6. Subset sizes are not reported.
  - Source: [DPC, arXiv 2604.15163](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15163)
- **SIRIUS-SQL.** The trained pairwise RM used alone loses to MV, overall and on challenging questions (numbers in the table in Section 2). "Merge" (S_maj + S_rm) beats MV by +0.65. — [SIRIUS-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01246)
- **R³-SQL.**
  - Two qualitative cases (BIRD-dev Q196, Q1527) where "the largest incorrect group ranks first" under the pointwise ranker, and R³ promotes the smaller correct group.
  - Confidence gating (τ) is used because "negative–negative pairings ... can accidentally elevate incorrect candidate groups over correct ones, which is a practical source of false positives".
  - No aggregate counts.
  - Source: [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **XiYan-SQL.** SC "cannot adequately handle situations where there is a lack of majority consensus, and even the most consistent result is not always the correct case". Its fix still seeds the selector with cluster-size order, and random order (s1) underperformed MV at pl=10 (68.19 vs 70.21). — [XiYan-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04701)
- **CHASE-SQL.** In 10.4% of BIRD-dev questions "a correct candidate existed but [was] not chosen". No breakdown by whether the correct candidate was the majority. — [CHASE-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01943)
- **CSC-SQL.** Qualitative only. In Case 82 the top-1 SC answer was wrong, the top-2 right, and the merge correct. Failed cases 15 and 590 show the merge producing a wrong answer. No aggregate flip rates. — [CSC-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13271)
- **GradeSQL.** Its largest gain over MV comes where MV is weakest: on the OmniSQL-32B pool, ORM 69.30 vs MV 63.95 (+5.35). On the default pool the gain is +1.95. — [GradeSQL v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01308v2)
- **MaP-SQL.** Motivated by "a larger incorrect group can dominate a smaller correct one" but has no overturn analysis. Its order-consistency study (Table 6, n=8) reports: random 12.58%, plain listwise 18.98%, group-based permutation 29.04%, plus tie-break 33.17%. — [MaP-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00834)

### Inferences
- **DPC implied counts (my arithmetic).** Assume the majority-correct set is all questions SC gets right (≈232 of 500), and the majority-incorrect set is all others with a correct candidate (≈56 = pass@5 − SC). Then DPC gains about 12 questions and loses about 7. That is roughly 1.7 rescues per false override. These sizes are inferred; the paper does not give them, and the implied net (+5) is one question short of the reported +6.
- **No minority-targeted training data exists in the literature.** CHASE, Agentar, SIRIUS and R³ all build pairs from cluster representatives without vote-share information, so none of them oversamples "correct-but-outvoted" pairs. For our failure mode (1 of 4 correct, 2–3 agreeing on a wrong answer), the training set would need deliberately mined pairs where the wrong side had the larger cluster in the generator's own sampling. Section 9 develops this.
- **Combining with votes is the safer design.** SIRIUS, XiYan, R³ and Contextual all add a vote or likelihood prior. But a prior that favours size works against exactly our minority-correct cases, so the weight needs tuning on held-out pools.

### Gaps
- No paper on a trained selector reports a confusion matrix of (majority correct or wrong) × (selector agrees or overrides).
- DPC does not give subset sizes. The results are for a training-free method at N=5.

## 7. Error taxonomies and error-detection datasets that could supply or guide negatives

### Takeaway
NL2SQL-Bugs and SQLens are the most useful guides for generating targeted negatives:
- **NL2SQL-Bugs.** 9 categories and 31 subcategories; 2,018 expert labels on BIRD-dev SQL. LLMs detect errors about 75% of the time but identify the type less than 50% of the time.
- **SQLens.** 9 database-side signals, including suboptimal join tree, incorrect join predicate, incorrect GROUP BY and abnormal result, plus 5 LLM-side signals.

Mutation and near-miss recipes come from XiYan (LLM "controlled modifications"), Graph-Reward-SQL (AND/OR and comparison-operator swaps) and ExCoT (small edit distance). Label noise is substantial: 10% wrong gold in CHASE's BIRD-dev analysis, and a 52.8% annotation error rate in BIRD Mini-Dev.

### Cited Findings
- **NL2SQL-Bugs taxonomy** (9 categories / 31 subcategories):
  - Attribute: Mismatch, Redundancy, Missing.
  - Table: Mismatch, Redundancy, Missing, Join Condition Mismatch, Join Type Mismatch.
  - Value: Mismatch, Data Format Mismatch.
  - Operator: Comparison, Logical.
  - Condition: Explicit Missing, Mismatch, Redundancy; Implicit Missing.
  - Function: Aggregate, Window, Date/Time, Conversion, Math, String, Conditional.
  - Clause: Missing, Redundancy.
  - Subquery: Missing, Mismatch, Partial Query.
  - Other: ASC/DESC, DISTINCT, Others.
  - Source: [NL2SQL-Bugs, arXiv 2503.11984](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11984)
- **NL2SQL-Bugs data.**
  - 2,018 instances: 1,019 correct, 999 incorrect.
  - Built on the BIRD **dev** set, with SQL from RESDSQL, CodeS and CHESS(GPT-4o).
  - Inter-annotator agreement: Cohen's κ = 0.78.
  - Binary detection accuracy: GPT-4o 76.81; Claude-3.5-Sonnet 76.36; Gemini-2.0-Flash 76.81; DeepSeek-V3 75.02; Qwen2.5-72B 74.38; GPT-4o-mini 71.56; average 75.16.
  - Type-level accuracy is below 50%. It is presented as an evaluation benchmark, not training data.
  - Source: [NL2SQL-Bugs](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11984); [project page](https://nl2sql-bugs.github.io/)
- **SQLens (NeurIPS 2025).**
  - Its taxonomy has three classes: question ambiguity, data ambiguity and semantic misalignment.
  - Database-side signals: Suboptimal Join Tree, Incorrect Join Predicate, Empty Predicate, Abnormal Result, Incorrect Filter in Subquery, Incorrect GROUP BY, Unnecessary Subquery, Value Ambiguity, Table Similarity.
  - LLM-side signals: Evidence Violation, Insufficient Evidence, Incorrect Question Clause Linking, Column Ambiguity, LLM Self-Check.
  - Signals are aggregated by weak supervision into a classifier.
  - Error-detection F1 on BIRD for CHESS output: 48.17 vs 23.81 for the best LLM self-evaluation. EX for CHESS rises 67.91 → 69.74.
  - Source: [SQLens, arXiv 2506.04494](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04494)
- **SQLCriticBench.** 3,019 samples with clause-level critique labels. WHERE clauses had the highest critique-error rate (43.75%). — [SQLCritic v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07996v4)
- **Chen et al. 2023 (Findings EMNLP 2023).** A parser-independent error detector (code LM plus GNN) "trained on realistic parsing errors collected from a cross-domain setting". RuCo-C used this error data to build about 40K Spider SFT examples. — [arXiv 2305.13683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13683); [RuCo-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22258)
- **Recipes for constructed negatives.**
  - XiYan: LLM "controlled modifications" that introduce subtle errors into correct queries. — [XiYan-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04701)
  - Graph-Reward-SQL: rule-based non-equivalent rewrites (AND/OR swaps, comparison-operator swaps) and equivalent rewrites (IN-clause). — [Graph-Reward-SQL](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12380)
  - ExCoT: on-policy pairs with small edit distance. — [ExCoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19988)
  - Contextual: highest-likelihood wrong candidates. — [Contextual blog](https://contextual.ai/blog/open-sourcing-the-best-local-text-to-sql-system)
  - R³: top-15 highest-scoring wrong candidates. — [R³-SQL PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25325)
- **Label noise.**
  - The UIUC audit finds annotation-error rates of **52.8% in BIRD Mini-Dev** and **62.8% in Spider 2.0-Snow**.
  - Correcting a BIRD Dev subset changes agent ranks by −9 to +9 positions.
  - Source: [Pervasive Annotation Errors, arXiv 2601.08778 (VLDB 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778)
- **New overlap finding.** BIRD **dev** includes `formula_1` and `european_football_2`. — [BIRD Mini-Dev README (third-party HF upload)](https://huggingface.co/datasets/epiphanian/bird_mini_dev/blob/main/README.md)
- **The matching local databases.** The local Spider2-Lite `EU_soccer` has tables Country, League, Match, Player, Player_Attributes, Team and Team_Attributes (the European Soccer Database layout). `f1` has Ergast-style tables: circuits, constructors, drivers, races, results, lap_times, pit_stops, qualifying, seasons and others. — [local Spider2 schema folder](/Users/deepikapalagani/Documents/nl2sql-databao/Spider2/spider2-lite/resource/databases/sqlite/)

### Inferences
- **Our error classes mapped to these taxonomies (my mapping, not from any paper).**

  | Our error class | NL2SQL-Bugs subcategories | SQLens signals |
  |---|---|---|
  | Wrong grain | Aggregate function; Clause Missing/Redundancy (GROUP BY) | Incorrect GROUP BY |
  | Wrong denominator | Aggregate/Math function; Implicit Condition Missing | – |
  | Join fan-out | Join Condition/Type Mismatch; Table Redundancy | Suboptimal Join Tree; Incorrect Join Predicate |
  | Overlapping status categories | Explicit/Implicit Condition Mismatch; Value Mismatch | Value Ambiguity |
  | Name instead of ID | Attribute Mismatch | Column Ambiguity |
  | Answer shape | Attribute Redundancy/Missing; Clause Missing (ORDER BY/LIMIT) | – |
  | Ties at a cutoff | Clause-related (LIMIT); "Others" | – |
  | Formatting / labels | Data Format Mismatch | – |

  None of the taxonomies has explicit categories for ties at a cutoff or for result-shape conventions. Those classes will need our own mutation rules.
- **More overlap.** NL2SQL-Bugs and SQLens's BIRD evaluations use BIRD dev, so they include `formula_1` ≈ f1 and `european_football_2` ≈ EU_soccer. Exclude those databases if any of this data is used for training. Spider-1's `soccer_1` is also the European Soccer DB.
- **Our scorer and BIRD-style labels disagree.** Training labels from BIRD-style result-set matching penalize extra columns, which our Spider2 scorer tolerates (CLAUDE.md: "extra predicted columns are tolerated"; condition_cols and ignore_order apply). Relabelling candidates with a Spider2-style comparator would avoid teaching the judge to punish answers that our evaluation accepts.

### Gaps
- No dataset found labels "ties at cutoff" or "label/format convention" errors.
- NL2SQL-Bugs' data license is not stated; only the project website is CC BY-SA 4.0.

## 9. Cross-system comparison and implications for building a ~9B pairwise judge's training data

### Takeaway
Across 14 systems, the consensus recipe is:
1. Sample K candidates per training question.
2. Label each by execution match.
3. Pair or group correct against incorrect across result clusters.
4. Mine hard negatives by likelihood or by the score of an existing ranker.
5. Train a 7B–32B Qwen model.

On training method, SFT gives the smallest pair-accuracy gains. GRPO with a swap-consistency reward gives the largest (R³: +11.2 pair-accuracy points), yet only about +1.6 EX. End-to-end gains over MV are +1.2 to +4.2, and a judge used alone can lose to MV.

### Inferences
These are synthesis recommendations, not measured findings.

- **Source data (contamination-safe).**
  - Use BIRD train and Spider train minus the overlapping databases. That means dropping movie_3, retail_world, works_cycles and soccer_2016 from BIRD train, and sakila_1, chinook_1, store_1, soccer_1, formula_1 and baseball_1 from Spider train.
  - Do not use BIRD-dev-derived sets (NL2SQL-Bugs) without dropping formula_1 and european_football_2.
  - Add synthetic data (SynSQL-2.5M, as OmniSQL does; R³ attributes its listwise ranker's better OOD behaviour to that broad corpus).
  - Never use the 135 Spider2-Lite questions or their gold.
- **Generate candidates on-policy.** Run our own Qwen3.6-35B agent (and the 9B, if it will generate) on those training questions with pass@K sampling. LEVER's cross-LLM table shows off-policy verifiers lose up to 7.7 points, and MaP shows a BIRD reward model applied to new pools hovering around MV.
- **Pair construction aimed at our failure mode.**
  - Mine questions where the correct answer is in a minority cluster (1-of-4 or 1-of-K) and over-weight (correct minority, wrong majority) pairs.
  - Keep some (correct majority, wrong minority) pairs at their natural rate so the judge does not learn "always pick the odd one out". DPC's −3 points on majority-correct questions is the cost to budget against.
  - Following SIRIUS, include both-wrong pairs so the judge learns to say "neither".
- **Hard negatives, in three tiers.**
  1. Natural on-policy wrong answers of high likelihood (Contextual) or high judge score (R³).
  2. Near-miss mutations of correct SQL (XiYan, Graph-Reward-SQL) targeted at our eight error classes, via the Section 7 mapping.
  3. Mutations aimed at "result shape" and "ties at a cutoff", which no public taxonomy covers.
- **Labels.** Use a Spider2-style comparator: gold columns must be matched, extra columns tolerated, condition_cols and ignore_order respected. Filter or flag noisy gold, given CHASE's 10% wrong gold and the 52.8% Mini-Dev error rate.
- **Order bias.** Train both orders of every pair (SIRIUS, CHASE). If using RL, add the swap-consistency reward (R³: +11.89 pp order robustness; GRPO + consistency 78.86 vs SFT 67.66 pair accuracy).
- **What to expect.** Literature gains over MV are +1.2 to +4.2 EX on BIRD with 17–64 candidates. Our zero-shot thinking judge already closes about 41% of the MV-to-oracle gap on our pools, more than any published trained selector does on BIRD (15–35%). A fine-tuned 9B judge should therefore be compared against the 35B thinking judge, not against MV. CHASE's tuned Gemma-2-9B only matched untuned Gemini-Pro.

### Gaps
- None of the 14 systems publishes per-error-type pair accuracy, so it is unknown which error classes trained judges actually learn to catch.
- No paper ablates oversampling minority-correct pairs, or measures a trained judge's false-override rate.
